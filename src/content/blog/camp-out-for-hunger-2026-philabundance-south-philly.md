South Philly lit up the sky. A drone show hung over Xfinity Mobile Arena and drew a CAMP OUT can in lights, big enough to read from the far end of the parking lot, and I stood there with my phone up like everybody else. Down on the ground there were food trucks, live music and a crowd carrying bags of groceries. This was Food Trucks & Fireworks Night, presented by Five Below, at Preston & Steve's Camp Out for Hunger 2026. It took over Lot G at Xfinity Mobile Arena, next to Stateside Live! on Pattison Avenue, in the South Philadelphia Sports Complex. I am Harry Hayman, and I went for the spectacle and stayed for what the spectacle was for. People came to hand food to Philabundance, to help feed our neighbors long after the last firework faded. That is the part I want to write about, because it is the part that should outlast the night.

## What Camp Out for Hunger actually is

If you have only seen it from the highway or on a screen, here is what it is. [Philabundance describes Camp Out for Hunger](https://www.philabundance.org/camp-out-for-hunger/) as the nation's largest single site food drive, run with WMMR's Preston & Steve for communities facing food insecurity in Southeastern Pennsylvania and Southern New Jersey. The same page calls 2026 the 29th year in a row.

It is not a one night party. The 2026 event ran Monday, November 16 through Friday, November 20, in the parking lot of Xfinity Mobile Arena, from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. each day, with a daily broadcast and an open invitation for everyone to bring food. The ask is simple: canned goods and other non perishables. Companies can also join a Business Challenge, competing to donate the most funds and food, and anyone who cannot come in person can give online.

I like how plain that is. Nobody needs a ticket or a title. You bring what you have, hand it over and watch it add up.

## What a week in a parking lot adds up to

The numbers are the part most people do not know, and they are bigger than the crowd makes you guess.

[Radio Ink reported](https://radioink.com/2023/11/26/wmmrs-preston-steve-camp-out-fills-philly-food-pantry/) that the 26th annual Camp Out, held November 13 to 17, 2023, amassed 1.7 million pounds of food and more than $948,000 in cash donations for Philabundance. Two years later, [Philabundance announced the 2025 final numbers](https://www.philabundance.org/2025-camp-out-for-hunger-announces-final-numbers/): over 1.8 million pounds of food and over $1.6 million, which it described as 27 percent more in donations than in previous years. That was the 28th year Philabundance partnered with Preston & Steve, and many of the 2025 gifts were made in honor of WMMR host Pierre Robert, who died on October 29, 2025.

Think about that for a second. Millions of pounds of food, gathered in a parking lot, by people who showed up with bags from their own pantries and grocery runs. I have seen plenty of civic efforts that took years to move a fraction of that. The lesson I take from it is not that drives are enough. It is that the appetite to help is already here, and the work is to point it somewhere that lasts. Philadelphia knows how to turn out when somebody gives it a place, a date and a reason.

## Where the food goes next

A drive like this only matters if the food reaches somebody, so I wanted to understand the second half of the story too. [Philabundance says it works with more than 300 community partners](https://www.philabundance.org/?p=20030), including shelters, other nonprofits, emergency kitchens, senior centers and schools, and that its service area covers nine counties: Bucks, Chester, Delaware, Montgomery and Philadelphia in Pennsylvania, and Burlington, Camden, Gloucester and Salem in New Jersey.

So the cans from Lot G do not go to one pantry. They move out through a network, to the places that put food in people's hands in their own neighborhoods. That is why I get a little emotional about the volunteers unloading cars. They are the first link in a chain that runs to a school cafeteria in one county and a senior center in another.

The need behind that chain is large. In Philadelphia alone, [the City's Board of Health cites Feeding America data](https://www.phila.gov/2025-08-06-farmers-markets-promote-access-to-fresh-fruits-and-vegetables/) showing that more than 210,000 residents faced food insecurity in 2023, over 13 percent of the city, and that 25 percent of Philadelphia's children lack secure food access at home. Those are our neighbors. They are who the food trucks and fireworks were really for, and they are the reason a feel good night has to turn into a year round habit. A child who is hungry on a Tuesday in March does not care how good the fireworks were in November.

## The people who made the night

I want to say thank you properly, because a night like this is built by hands. Thank you to Preston & Steve, 93.3 WMMR, Philabundance, Stateside Live! and Five Below, who presented Food Trucks & Fireworks Night. Thank you to the food truck crews, to the volunteers unloading cars, and to everybody who showed up with a bag of groceries. You made the night what it was.

And a special thank you to Kim, Loree and Dorothy and everyone at Philabundance. The work they do does not stop when the broadcast ends. It is the same work on a Tuesday in February, when nobody is lighting up the sky.

## Why this is a Feed Philly Coalition story

I have spent years, through [Feed Philly Coalition](https://feedphillycoalition.org/), pushing Philadelphia to treat food as essential infrastructure. We take it for granted that a city needs working roads, clean water and power. I want us to say the same about food, and then act like it.

In practice, that means three things. Put more locally grown food on local tables. Make it easier to get good food in every neighborhood. And get the institutions with the biggest purchasing power, the ones that feed students, patients and employees every day, to buy more from the people who grow and make food right here.

Camp Out for Hunger answers an emergency, and it answers it beautifully. But a drive can only catch what falls through the gaps, and I would love for Philadelphia to build a food system with fewer gaps to fall through. When a city shows it can gather over a million pounds of food in a week, I do not hear charity alone. I hear proof of what people will do when the way to help is obvious and easy. Our job is to make the everyday system that obvious and easy too.

I want us to bring the same energy Philly brought to Pattison Avenue to that work all year long.

## You can still help

Camp Out for Hunger comes back every November, and any day of the year you can [give directly to Philabundance online](https://www.philabundance.org/camp-out-for-hunger/). Grab a few extra cans on your next grocery run. If your office can fill a box by Friday morning, fill the damn box.
